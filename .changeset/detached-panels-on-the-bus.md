The detached panels' windows are on the bus (ADR 0029, slice 4, second part), owned by the workbench (`bus::WORKBENCH_OWNER`). Detaching a panel is its window's `window.open` (`panel:<id>`); docking it back, by `attach_panel` or by moving it to an area as a dragged tab does, is its window's `window.reparent` into the main window; closing its window (its title bar, the system's close, `workbench.close_window`, or anyone asking `window.close` of it) is its `window.close`, and its panels dock back. The workbench's listener on the three points (`bus::WORKBENCH_WINDOW`) changes the layout whoever asked, before the registry holds or lets go of the window. A vetoed close, by the `workbench.close_window` step or the `window.close` step, leaves the registry and the layout as they were and cancels the system's close. Any other workbench change that adds or removes a detached window (a panel joining another window's tabs, a layout put in, panels declared again) asks the same requests in the call that changed the layout: the registry no longer catches up with the detached panels once per pass. While a window's content holds the main window, the detached panels' windows leave the registry, and come back with the shell. In the test kit, `MultiWindow` is the context's immediate renderer: a detached panel's window is a real viewport, its pass run with its own input inside the main window's pass that shows it (`MultiWindow::shown_immediate`), checked against the registry like any other.
